Rules & Etiquette.

The short version of pickleball basics and how we play at OCPC — everything a first-timer needs before stepping on court.

01

The Basics

01

Serve underhand, diagonally

Serves are hit underhand and must land in the opposite diagonal service box. You get one serve attempt (no second serve like tennis).

02

Two-bounce rule

After the serve, the ball must bounce once on each side before anyone can volley it (hit it out of the air). After that, you can volley freely — outside the kitchen.

03

No volleying in the kitchen

The "kitchen" is the 7-foot no-volley zone on each side of the net. You can stand in it, but you can't hit the ball out of the air while any part of you is touching it.

04

Points only on serve

In traditional scoring, only the serving side can score a point. Games are typically played to 11, win by 2.

05

Call your own lines

At the open-play level, players call their own in/out shots. Call it as you see it, and when in doubt, favor your opponent — it's part of the culture.

06

Doubles is standard

Most open play is doubles (2v2). Rotations mean you'll play with different partners throughout a session — that's the point.

Rule of Thumb

If you're not sure whether you're allowed to hit a shot, ask before you swing — regulars are always happy to walk new players through a rule mid-game. Nobody expects you to know everything on day one.

05

Court Etiquette

1

Rotate in, rotate out

Open play runs on rotation — after your game, clear the court quickly so the next group can start. If there's a queue, sit out gracefully and wait your turn.

2

Return balls that roll onto your court

If a ball rolls in from another court, pause your play and return it once there's a break — don't just kick it aside.

3

Keep score out loud

Call the score before every serve so both sides are on the same page — it avoids disputes later in the game.

4

No coaching from the sidelines mid-game

Unless it's a beginner clinic, let players figure out their own shots during a game. Save the tips for after.

5

Good sportsmanship, always

Tap paddles after a game, thank your partner and opponents, and keep the energy positive — win or lose. That's the culture we're building.

6

Come prepared

Bring your own water, wear non-marking court shoes, and show up a few minutes early so rotations stay on schedule.
